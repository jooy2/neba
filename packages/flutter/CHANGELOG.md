# Changelog

The changes to `neba` on pub.dev, the Flutter package. The React package keeps its own changelog in [packages/react/CHANGELOG.md](https://github.com/jooy2/neba/blob/main/packages/react/CHANGELOG.md). Each package is released from a tag of its own, and they share version numbers from 1.21.0, the first Flutter release.

## vNext (2026--)

### Added

- **Neba UI for Flutter.** The first release of the Flutter package, at the version number of the React package's next release. It carries the same design language as the React package and is built on `package:flutter/widgets.dart` alone, with no dependencies.

- **The tokens of both themes, computed from the React stylesheet.** `NebaTokens.light()` and `NebaTokens.dark()` hold every neutral and every colour family as the stylesheet writes them, in `oklch()`, and derive the fills, washes, panels, hairlines and rings with the same `color-mix()` arithmetic. `copyWith` and `withFamily` bring a product's own colours, and the derived tokens follow.

- **`NebaProvider`, `NebaTheme` and `NebaDefaults`.** A widget works with no setup and follows the platform's brightness; `NebaProvider` sets defaults for a subtree, pins the colour scheme and the direction, and takes a product's own light and dark tokens. A platform high-contrast setting makes every sheet opaque, as the React package does under `prefers-reduced-transparency`.

- **The shared prop vocabulary and the size ladders.** `NebaSize`, `NebaColor`, `NebaDensity`, `NebaVariant`, `NebaOrientation`, `NebaSide`, `NebaAlign`, `NebaCorner`, `NebaLabelPlacement` and `NebaColorScheme` mean what they mean in the React package, and `NebaScale` holds the control heights, type sizes, icon sizes, padding, gaps and motion it uses. React's density `'default'` is `NebaDensity.standard`, because Dart reserves the word.

- **The words the widgets say on their own behalf, in nineteen languages.** English is built in; the other eighteen are constants in `package:neba/locales.dart` that an app registers with `registerNebaMessages('ko', ko)`, so a language that is never registered is never in the build. A tag is matched by script, then region, then language, and a widget speaks its own `locale`, then `NebaProvider`'s, then the app's. The tables are generated from the React package's, word for word.

- **The chart and code palettes.** `NebaTokens.chart` holds the eight series colours in the order they are handed out, the sequential and diverging ramps, and the ink a label wears on each colour as a `NebaInkTone`, so a product that changes `fg` or `surface` carries the labels along. `chartGrid`, `chartAxis`, `chartBaseline` and `chartGap` are a chart's chrome, and `NebaTokens.code` is a code block's `auto` palette. All of them are the React stylesheet's values, held to it by the same test as the rest.

- **`NebaTransition`**, the entrance a widget that displays something plays when it is first built: a fade, a grow, a slide, a zoom, a rotate, a blink or a reveal, with the React package's defaults, a `Duration` for the timing, a `Curve` for the easing, a repeat count and `alternate`. A reader who asked the platform for less motion sees the last frame at once. `NebaBox` takes it as `transition`.

- **`NebaBox`**, the plainest sheet: `solid`, `outline` and `text`, its corners and padding on the size ladder, a hairline in the colour's family and a sheet that stays undyed, and an elevation from 0 to 3.

- **`NebaContainer`**, the gutters around a page's content: padding on `NebaBox`'s steps, a `maxWidth` held to the measure ladder or a width of your own, centred or at the start. `NebaMeasure` is the shared type for that width, the React package's `NebaMeasure` without the CSS lengths.

- **`NebaGridContainer` and `NebaGrid`**, the twelve-column layout: a row that wraps, items that span a number of columns so the gutters line up from row to row, `NebaGridSpan.auto` and `NebaGridSpan.grow` for the widths a column count cannot reach, offsets of whole columns, and the row's `justifyContent`, `alignItems` and `alignContent` with an item's own `alignSelf`. `NebaJustifyContent`, `NebaAlignItems` and `NebaAlignSelf` join the shared types. A per-breakpoint map is the React package's alone.

- **`NebaFlex`**, a row or a column with a gutter on the spacing scale: `direction` and `reverse`, `rowSpacing` and `columnSpacing` over `spacing`, the row's `justifyContent`, `alignItems` and `alignContent`, `wrap` for a row that continues on the next line, and `inline` for one only as wide as its children. Stretched children are as thick as the thickest of them, as in CSS.

- **`NebaStack`**, things laid over each other so a set reads as a pile: `horizontal`, `vertical` or a fanned `diagonal`, an `overlap` and a `drop` that take exactly the room the pile covers, `front`, a `scaleStep` and an `opacityStep` for depth, `max`, `total` and `overflow` for the ones that did not fit, a `ring` in the page's surface colour that the first sheet in each item draws around its own corners, and a `transition` dealt with `stagger`, `durationStep` and `reverse`.

- **`NebaShow`** and **`NebaBreakpoint`**: a child shown from a floor, below a ceiling, or between the two, on the React package's five breakpoints, read off the window's width. A hidden child is not built, and `NebaFlex` and `NebaGridContainer` leave a hidden `NebaShow` out entirely, gap and all, as CSS leaves out `display: none`.

- **`NebaAspectRatio`**, a box that keeps a proportion whatever width it is given: it reserves the space before its content arrives, clips what runs past it, fits an `Image` child with a `BoxFit`, and rounds its corners to the radius ladder when asked.

- **`NebaScrollArea`**, a box with a scrollbar of its own: a rail on the size ladder in the colour's family that shows while the pointer is over the area or while it scrolls, `vertical`, `horizontal` or `both`, a `height` or a `maxHeight`, a `fade` at each edge with more beyond it, a focus stop with the arrows, the page keys, Home and End while it overflows, and a name in the `locale`'s words.

- **`NebaPortal`**, a child drawn in the app's root overlay, or the nearest one with `rootOverlay: false`, rather than where it was written, so it escapes a clip, a scroll or a sheet. It is built where it was written and keeps the theme and the `NebaProvider` defaults from there, takes no room in place, and `disabled` draws it in place instead.

- **`NebaTypography`**, text on the library's type scale: ten levels from `h1` to `overline` with the React package's sizes, leading ratios, tracking and weights, the heading level a screen reader hears and `headingLevel` to change it, a `color` that inherits when it is left out, `weight`, `align`, `lines` with an ellipsis, a `gutter`, a `style` laid over the level, a `transition`, and `NebaTypography.rich` for text in more than one style.

- **`NebaDivider`**, a rule between two things in its colour's hairline: horizontal or vertical, the room it is given or a `length` in logical pixels, a `thickness`, and a label set into it that splits the line in the middle or leaves a short stub at the `start` or the `end`, turning with a vertical rule. A `Text` label is what a screen reader hears.

- **`NebaVisuallyHidden`**, content a screen reader reads and the screen does not show: laid out at its own size in a box of none, painted transparent, out of the pointer's reach and still in the semantics tree with its actions; `visible` takes the hiding off, which is what a skip link needs.

- **`NebaSkeleton`**, a placeholder in the shape of what is on its way: a `line` the height of body text, `lines` of them with the last one shorter, a `rect` block or a `circle`, a highlight travelling across it towards the end of the line that turns into a slow pulse under reduced motion, and a `semanticLabel` that makes the one skeleton standing for a region a live region.

- **`NebaBlockquote`**, a passage quoted from somewhere else: a rule in the colour's accent down its start edge in every `variant`, `text` alone or on an `outline` or `solid` sheet, a quote mark at twice the text size that `icon` replaces or `null` takes away, and an `author` after an em dash a screen reader skips, followed by the `source`.

- **`NebaHighlight`**, the words a reader is looking for, marked inside a `Text`, a `Text.rich` or a `NebaTypography`: one term, `NebaHighlight.terms` tried longest first, or `NebaHighlight.pattern` with a `RegExp` used as written. A term ignores case unless `caseSensitive` and the accents of Latin, Greek and Cyrillic letters, so `jose` marks `José`; `wholeWord` keeps it to words of its own. Spans are walked into and keep their style and gestures. A `solid`, `outline` or `text` mark in a colour family, `warning` by default, is drawn behind its word a hair wider than it, a fragment to a line, so the text never moves; `underline` and `weight` add to any of them.

- **`NebaShortcut`**, a keyboard shortcut drawn as key caps: `keys` split on `+`, or `NebaShortcut.split` for a key that is itself a plus, with the aliases one key already has. `Mod` is Command on a Mac and Control everywhere else, `os` names a platform or reads the one the app runs on, macOS draws its modifiers as symbols in its own order and runs them together, and the others join their words with a plus unless `separator` says otherwise. Caps are one step down the control ladder in a monospaced face, `outline` and `secondary` by default, and a screen reader hears every key by its name.

- **`NebaTextLink`**, a link in a sentence or on its own, with no surface and no height of its own. The package opens nothing, so `onPressed` follows the link and `href` tells a screen reader and the browser where it goes, kept only for a relative address or an `http`, `https`, `mailto` or `tel` one. The line under it is drawn `always`, on `hover` or `none`, in part of the label's colour and in all of it under the pointer, under the text's box so it clears the descenders. With no `color` or `size` it takes the sentence's; `newTab` draws the external mark at the end of the last line and is announced as opening a new tab in the `locale`'s words, and `icon` and `showIcon` change the mark. Enter follows it and Space does not.

- **`NebaIcon`**, a glyph from any icon set on the library's size and colour axes: a square on the icon ladder of 14, 16, 20, 24 and 28 pixels, the size and the colour handed to the glyph through `IconTheme` and `DefaultTextStyle`, the colour of the text around it unless `color` names a family, out of the semantics tree unless a `semanticLabel` makes it an image, and a `transition` to arrive by.

- **`NebaAppLogo`**, a product's mark at a known size that is never an empty box: an `image`, a drawing as `child` that takes the colour around it, the initials of `name` on a tile, or with nothing else the name set as a logotype. `shape` draws it `bare`, on an `app` tile or a `circle` that the `variant` fills in the colour family, `padded` leaves the artwork 72% of the tile, `height` overrides the control height and `showName` sets the name beside the mark. It is one node named once for a screen reader, and `onPressed` makes the whole lockup a link with a safe `href`.

- **`NebaStatistic`**, a figure on a sheet with what it counts, a `prefix` and a `unit` on its baseline, and how it moved since `previousValue`: a chip with a rising or a falling arrow, or a dash when nothing moved by as much as it is written, as a percentage, an amount or `both`, in `success` when it went the way `betterWhen` says is good news and `danger` when it did not. A number is digit-grouped unless `format` writes it, a `String` or a widget is drawn as it is, and anything under the rest goes in `child`.

- **`NebaAnchor`**, the headings of the page being read with the reader's marked: each item names its heading by the `GlobalKey` of its widget, the list follows the scroll view those headings are in and marks the last heading past `offset`, the first while there is nothing to scroll and the last at the end, and a pressed row scrolls its heading to `offset`. `activeTarget` decides the mark while `onActiveChange` goes on reporting; `rail` draws a line down the leading edge lit beside the marked row, and `depth` indents a row.

- **`NebaTable`**, rows of records under a header on a sheet, generic over the row: a `NebaTableColumn` names a column, builds its cells or reads them from a row that is a `Map`, and takes a `width` and an `align`, and the columns without a width share the rest by their content. `striped`, `hoverable`, `onRowPressed` with each row a focus stop that answers Enter and Space and is ringed after a key, a `caption`, an `empty` line across the width, and a `stickyHeader` over rows that scroll inside `maxHeight`. A screen reader reads it as a table.

- **`NebaRating`**, a score as a row of stars: a set of radios with one focus stop whose arrow keys move, choose and wrap, steps of `precision` that split a star into press targets, the score the pointer would give shown before it is pressed, a press on the chosen score clearing it unless `clearable` is off, and a fill that travels by width across a star in the `warning` family. `icon` and `emptyIcon` replace the glyphs, `count` sets the stars, `disabled` takes the input away and `readOnly` makes it one picture of a score.

- **`NebaPagination`**, a row of page numbers as buttons with the current one filled: the pages either side of it and at each end, an ellipsis for a run left out that never stands for a single page, a row that keeps its number of slots as the page moves, steps back and forth and to the ends that stay in the focus order when they have nowhere to go, and a status read politely saying which page of how many. It draws nothing for fewer than two pages; `onPageChange` goes to the page, as the package opens nothing itself.

- **`NebaCarousel`**, slides shown one at a time in a frame as tall as the tallest of them: a strip that snaps a slide at a time under a swipe, arrows over the frame, a dot per slide under it and the arrow keys while the strip holds the focus, `loop` or arrows that go inert at the ends, and `value` and `onValueChange` for a strip something else drives. `autoPlay` turns it every `interval`, holds under the pointer, with the focus inside, in the background and while it is scrolled out of sight, never starts for a reader who asked for less motion, and draws a button that stops it; the slide showing is announced whenever the reader moved it.

- **`NebaList`** and **`NebaListItem`**, a stack of rows of the same shape on a `NebaBox` sheet that stays undyed, `outline` by default, with `size`, `density` and the colour family set once on the list for every row. A row holds a `startIcon`, a label cut short on one line, a muted `description` under it and an `endIcon`; `onPressed` makes it a button, or a link with a safe `href`, and an `action` sits beside that pressable area rather than inside it. `selected` tints the row in the family, `disabled` turns it grey, `spacing` stands the tiles apart on the spacing scale, and `dividers` rules between the rows, which then square off and reach both edges with the ring drawn inside them. A screen reader hears a list of items, and a chosen row as selected.

- **`NebaDataList`** and **`NebaDataListItem`**, things and what they are called: a muted label one step down the type scale and its value, any widget, which keeps its own width. `horizontal` lays every label in one column, as wide as the widest label up to half the list unless `labelWidth` says otherwise, with each label on its value's baseline; `vertical` stacks a label over its value, nearer its own value than the pair before it. `dividers` draws the hairline between pairs, never between a label and its value, and `density` changes only the gaps. A screen reader hears each pair as one item, its label and then its value.

- **`NebaTimeline`** and **`NebaTimelineItem`**, steps in the order they happen in, numbered by the timeline so `active` can say how far the sequence has got: every step before it complete, it current, the rest upcoming, with `status` and `color` to override one step. A complete step's bullet is filled in its family, the current one's carries a halo round it and an upcoming one is an empty ring, each with a number or an icon in it or none, and the line to the next step is `solid`, `dashed`, `dotted` or `none`, coloured by whether the step it leaves was reached. A `title` and its `meta` share a baseline and wrap, the body sits under them in the muted ink, and `orientation` runs the steps down the page or shares the width across it. A screen reader hears a list of the steps in order, each finished or waiting one saying so in the `locale`'s words or in `labels`, and the current one selected.

- **`NebaBreadcrumb`** and **`NebaBreadcrumbItem`**, the trail of pages above the one being read. The last step is the current page, text a screen reader hears as selected, unless a step's `current` says otherwise; a step with `onPressed` is a button, or a link with a safe `href`, tinted in the family under the pointer, and `newTab` adds "(opens in a new tab)" to what a screen reader hears. `NebaBreadcrumbSeparator` draws a chevron, an arrow, a slash, a dot or a widget between the steps, the two that point turned back right to left. Past `maxItems` the middle folds behind a `…`, keeping `itemsBeforeCollapse` and `itemsAfterCollapse` at the ends, which unfolds when pressed and hands the focus to the first step it hid, folds again when the steps change, and stays a mark with `expandable: false`. The trail is a navigation region named in the `locale`'s words or by `label`, holding a list of its steps. `structuredData`, `baseUrl`, `keepMounted`, `render`, `target` and `rel` are the web's alone.

- **`NebaProgressLinear`**, progress as a bar that fills: a groove in the colour's family whose thickness is on the size ladder or a `thickness` in logical pixels, a `value` between `min` and `max` held to the range and eased to each new reading over the fill's duration, and a `label` with, when `showValue`, the reading at the end of the row above it, a whole percentage of the range unless a `format` function writes it. Without a `value` it is indeterminate: a segment crosses the groove towards the end of the line, painted further along on each frame rather than moved, and the whole groove pulses instead under reduced motion. A screen reader hears the reading as the bar's value, or a busy indicator while there is none, named by a `Text` label or by `semanticLabel`.

- **`NebaProgressCircular`**, progress as a ring that fills, for where there is no room for a bar: 14 to 32 logical pixels across, just under the control ladder at every size so it drops into a row of controls without making it taller, a wash for the ring and the family's accent for the arc, which starts at twelve o'clock and closes the gap clockwise as the reading climbs, and a `thickness` for the stroke, held to half the radius. The `label` and, when `showValue`, the reading sit beside the ring rather than inside it, and a long label is cut short where the room runs out. Without a `value` a quarter of the ring turns every 900 milliseconds, painted at each angle rather than rotated, or every 2.4 seconds under reduced motion.

- **`NebaProgressBox`**, progress as a row of acrylic plates, for a loading state on a Neba surface: `count` plates, four by default and never fewer than one, on the size ladder from 8 to 20 logical pixels square, each a wash with the plate's top light. With a `value` they fill from the start edge and the leading one partly, each easing to its own share, so four plates still show 30%; without one they light in turn, each 120 milliseconds behind the one before, taking the fill and the full hairline of a solid plate and giving them back without ever moving, at half the speed under reduced motion. The `label` and the reading sit above the row, which is as wide as the plates or as that row, whichever is wider.

- **`NebaMeter`** and **`NebaThreshold`**, how much of something there is on a scale known in advance: the groove a `NebaProgressLinear` cuts, on the same ladder, with a required `value` that is never indeterminate and never moves on its own, a `label` and the reading as a percentage or through a `format` function, and `thresholds`, points on the scale where the bar takes another colour family, read in the order given, with the fill easing to its new colour and the track taking its new wash at once.

- **`NebaButton`**, a control that runs an action: `solid`, `outline` and `text` in the six colour families on the size ladder, padding by `density`, an `elevation` that goes up a level on hover and down one on a press, the pointer spotlight and the release afterglow, `startIcon` and `endIcon` at 1.2 times the label, `loading` with a turning ring, `readOnly`, `disabled` and `focusableWhenDisabled`, `fullWidth`, and a `semanticLabel` for a button with only an icon. With no `onPressed` it is disabled, as every Flutter button is. A label stays on one line and, held narrower than itself, ends in an ellipsis.

- **`NebaButtonGroup`**, buttons that read as one cut piece: the corners facing a neighbour squared off, a single shared hairline between outline buttons, the button under the pointer or holding the focus drawn over its neighbours so its ring is never covered, `horizontal` or `vertical` with a vertical group as wide as its widest button, and `fullWidth`, which shares the width evenly unless a label needs more. `variant`, `size`, `color`, `density`, `elevation` and `disabled` are set once for the set, and a `NebaButton` takes its own argument, then its group's, then `NebaProvider`'s; its `disabled` is nullable for that reason.

- **`NebaIconButton`**, a round button holding a single glyph, with a `label` it requires because a drawing has no name of its own. Everything but the shape is `NebaButton`'s, the turning ring in the glyph's place included, and in a `NebaButtonGroup` the corners that face a neighbour are still squared off.

- **`NebaToggle`** and **`NebaToggleGroup`**, a button that stays down and a set of them that shares one state. Off is neutral, with muted ink on an undyed plate, and on is the colour family whichever variant was asked for, at the same depth. A toggle is controlled with `pressed` and `onPressedChange` or keeps its own state from `defaultPressed`; in a group the group owns the value as a list, `multiple` decides whether more than one can be on, the set is one stop in the focus order with the arrow keys, Home and End between its toggles, and it joins them as `NebaButtonGroup` joins buttons.

- **`NebaSegmentedButton`** and **`NebaSegment`**, two or more choices in one pill with exactly one taken, generic over the type of a segment's `value`. A tile slides behind the chosen segment by its rectangle, never by a transform, appears in place on the first choice, goes for a value no segment carries, and follows a resize without travelling. `solid` and `outline` sit in a frosted trough and `text` in none, on the control heights with padding by `density`; `disabled` drops the colour family, one `NebaSegment` can be disabled alone, `readOnly` keeps the choice and drains the saturation, and `fullWidth` shares the width evenly. A screen reader hears a radio group: the set is one stop in the focus order, and the arrow keys move between its segments and choose as they go.

- **`NebaTextField`**, a field that takes a line of text or several. The label, the description and the error stand in one column that a screen reader hears as one field, and an `error` turns the whole field over to the `danger` family. `solid`, `outline` and `text` keep the sheet undyed and put the colour in the edge, the ring flush with it and the caret; the height is a floor, so text set larger grows the field. `labelPlacement` puts the label above the field, in a notch in its top edge with the edge drawn around it, or inside it until it takes the focus or a value, travelling on its position and its size. `startIcon`, `endIcon`, `loading` with a turning ring, `multiline` with a minimum of `rows`, `readOnly`, `disabled`, `fullWidth`, the pointer's light at half strength and out while the reader types, and `shortcuts` as a map of `ShortcutActivator`s. It is an `EditableText` underneath, so `controller`, `focusNode`, `onChanged`, `onSubmitted`, `keyboardType`, `obscureText`, `autofillHints`, `inputFormatters` and `maxLength` are the ones any Flutter field takes, and `initialValue` is React's `defaultValue`. A touch screen shows no selection handles and no copy-and-paste toolbar yet.

- **`NebaCheckbox`**, a single yes or no: a frosted square beside its `label`, with a `description` under the label and an `error` under both, centred on the label's first line however far the label wraps. It fills with the colour family when ticked, with no plate, and the tick inside is drawn along its own length as it arrives and taken back the same way; `indeterminate` draws a dash for a parent whose children disagree. Controlled with `checked` and `onCheckedChange` or kept from `defaultChecked`, on the tick ladder `NebaScale` now holds, with `readOnly`, `disabled`, an `error` or `invalid` that turns it to `danger`, and a press on the label or the gap beside the square. A screen reader hears one checkbox, ticked, clear or mixed, named by its label.

- **`NebaCheckboxGroup`**, a set of checkboxes that answer one question, any number at once: one value for the set as a list of the ticked checkboxes' `value`s, controlled or kept from `defaultValue`, a `label` and a `description` above the options and an `error` under them, in a column or a wrapping row at gaps on the size ladder or a `spacing` of its own. `size`, `color` and `readOnly` reach every checkbox that does not set its own, and `disabled` and an invalid state reach all of them. With `allValues`, the checkbox with `parent` ticks every option, draws a dash while only some are, and from a mix ticks all, clears all and brings the mix back, leaving a disabled option as it was.

- **`NebaRadioGroup`** and **`NebaRadio`**, a set of options where exactly one is chosen, generic over the type of an option's `value`. Each option is a round frosted ring beside its `label` and `description` that fills with the set's colour family when chosen, with a dot grown into it from nothing by its size; the set has a `label` and a `description` above the options and an `error` under them, runs in a column or a wrapping row at the gaps `NebaCheckboxGroup` uses, and holds `size`, `color`, `disabled` and `readOnly` for every option. Controlled with `value` and `onValueChange` or kept from `defaultValue`. A screen reader hears a radio group; the set is one stop in the focus order, at the chosen option or the first, and the arrow keys move between the options and choose as they go, wrapping at the ends and passing over a disabled one.

- **`NebaSwitch`**, an on/off that takes effect the moment it moves: a pill-shaped track beside its `label`, a groove with a hairline in the colour family while off and filled with the family while on, with no plate, and a white thumb one level up that travels to the end of the line by its offset rather than a transform, at once for a reader who asked for less motion. The row makes room above and below a track taller than the label's first line, so it stays centred on that line and inside the row. `labelSide` puts the label before the track with `NebaSwitchLabelSide.start`, the track at the end of a stretched row, and `checked`, `onCheckedChange`, `defaultChecked`, `readOnly`, `disabled`, an `error` or `invalid` that turns it to `danger`, and the groove laid on the page under reduced transparency complete it. A screen reader hears one switch, on or off, named by its label.

- **`NebaChip`**, a compact token for a tag, a filter or a status: one step down the control ladder, so a `md` chip is 26 pixels tall, in `solid`, `outline` and `text` across the six colour families, with `startIcon`, `endIcon` and a `count` on a plate of its own. `onPressed` makes the label a button and `onDelete` adds a × beside it, each with its own focus stop; the × is named after a `Text` label in the `locale`'s words, or by `deleteLabel`, and takes a press over the chip's whole height. `selected` deepens the surface without changing the colour family, `disabled` turns it grey, and a long label is cut short with an ellipsis. There is no `href`: a chip that leads somewhere calls the router from `onPressed`.

- **`NebaAvatar`**, a picture of a person or a thing that is never an empty box: an `ImageProvider` as `image`, faded up over what stands in for it once it has decoded, with `child`, `initials` or the initials of `name` standing in until then, and for good when the picture fails, and a silhouette when there is nothing to go on. The initials are the first character of the first word and of the last, a whole grapheme each. A `circle` or `square` crop, `solid`, `outline` and `text` on the control heights, an `elevation`, a `delay` before the stand-in is drawn, and `onLoadingStatusChange`. Given a `name`, a screen reader hears the name rather than the initials, and an empty `semanticLabel` makes the avatar silent.

- **`NebaBadge`**, a small mark in the corner of something else: a widget `content`, a dot when there is none, or `NebaBadge.count`, which caps at `max` with a `+` and hides a zero unless `showZero`. It hangs half outside the corner `placement` names and tucks in further on a round `overlap`, leaves what it sits on laid out as it was, keeps its room while `invisible`, and says its `semanticLabel` rather than a bare number.

- **`NebaCard`**, a `NebaBox` with a `title`, a `subtitle`, a `headerAction` pinned to the end of the title's row, a body and a `footer` laid out on it, each built only when it is given, on the sheet type ladder that `NebaScale` now holds for every sheet. `dividers` separates the sections with a hairline in the colour's family and moves the padding onto each section. Every axis of the box passes through.

- **`NebaPanes` and `NebaPane`**, a box split into regions with a draggable bar between each pair: `horizontal` or `vertical`, laid out from the end under a right-to-left direction, and nested for a split inside a split. A `NebaPaneSize` is a percentage or a length in logical pixels, for a pane's `defaultSize`, `minSize` and `maxSize`; a length becomes a share when the split is first laid out, and one pane's floor is its neighbour's ceiling. A bar is a hairline in a track on the size ladder, grabbed across 24 pixels, lit in the colour's family under the pointer and while it is dragged. A press on it claims the pointer, so a scroll view around it stays put, follows the pointer inside a scaled ancestor and leaves the focus where it was; from the keyboard it takes the focus and moves 16 pixels with each arrow key.
